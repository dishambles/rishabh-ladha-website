1 Apr 2026 · Note

The manager who never leaves the office

In a large field sales organisation I spent time with recently, the strongest performers could each point to one manager who once went into the field with them. That is where their coaching actually happened.

Teams start to slide when managers stop training on the job. Leadership turns administrative, and the manager’s main tool becomes a CRM dashboard and the question “why are your numbers down?”

The managers whose teams keep improving run structured huddles that walk through wins and losses, use market data to choose where to focus, rehearse with mock calls and role plays, and make recognition visible. They run coaching as a weekly system.

26 Sep 2023 · Summary

What makes sales coaching work

My notes on Ambition’s Winner’s Guide to Sales Coaching. Coaching usually fails for two reasons: managers don’t have the time, and many were never taught how to coach. Fix both first. Take admin off managers’ plates, define the core competencies, give them a framework, and hold them to a cadence.

Then coach what reps control. Pick three or four daily activities, tie each one to a sales objective so reps see why it matters, and run one-on-ones on those numbers. Leave high performers alone. As Mike Weinberg puts it, start with results, then look at the pipeline, and ask about activity only when both are weak.

Finally, share positive feedback in public, block coaching time on the calendar, and get aspiring managers into the field early.
